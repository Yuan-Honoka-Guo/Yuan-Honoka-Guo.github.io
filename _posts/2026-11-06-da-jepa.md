<p class="da-intro">What if a world model could imagine a few future moments together, fill in their details, and then move on to the next part of the future?</p>

That was the question behind **Diffusion Autoregressive JEPA (DA-JEPA)**, a project I worked on during the summer of 2026. Built on LeWorldModel (LeWM), it replaced next-latent prediction with a hybrid: autoregression between temporal chunks, and iterative masked prediction inside each chunk.

## The idea I wanted to test

A latent world model encodes observations into representations and predicts how those representations change under actions. Those imagined futures can then help a planner choose actions toward a goal.

DA-JEPA kept that setup, but changed the unit of prediction. Instead of committing to one future state at a time, it worked with a block of future latent states. The hope was that modeling a short future jointly would improve temporal consistency and reduce the effect of accumulating local prediction errors. Those were hypotheses to test.

<div class="da-grid" markdown="1">
<section markdown="1">
### Between chunks: continue the trajectory

Take the latest latent history and the next block of candidate actions. Generate a chunk of future states, append it to the history, and repeat until the planning horizon is covered.
</section>
<section markdown="1">
### Within a chunk: fill the masked future

Start with masked latent slots. A conditional Transformer predicts their continuous values and reveals positions over several rounds. The balanced reveal schedule fills temporal anchors before the positions in between.
</section>
</div>

The implementation used masked latent prediction rather than pixel generation. The encoder and predictor were trained together with a denoising loss on masked positions plus **SIGReg**, the representation regularizer retained from LeWM.

<div class="da-equation" role="math" aria-label="Training loss equals masked latent prediction loss plus lambda times SIGReg regularization">L = L<sub>masked latent prediction</sub> + &lambda; L<sub>SIGReg</sub></div>

The word “diffusion” here refers to the masking-and-revealing procedure. The archived sampler initializes masked slots and follows a fixed reveal order; it does not establish diverse stochastic futures. Likewise, “hierarchical” describes the chunk and refinement levels, without a separately learned high-level planner.

## From the sketch to an experiment

The project reached a working implementation, training runs, and downstream planning evaluations. The implementation added an action-conditioned chunk predictor, a chunked rollout path, and a masked training objective while preserving the original LeWM path.

The saved configuration used a ViT-tiny encoder, 192-dimensional embeddings, three context states, and four predicted states per chunk. The archived planning evaluations covered **reaching (DMC), cube manipulation (OGB), PushT, and TwoRoom navigation**.

For the evaluations below, the saved settings specify 50 episodes, seed 42, and a cross-entropy method (CEM) planner with 300 candidates, 30 optimization steps, and 30 elites. The planner used a horizon of five action blocks, with five environment steps per block, and an evaluation budget of 50 environment steps.

PushT remained near zero success. A model can get better at its training objective while still being poor at the task the planner needs it to solve.

## What remains unresolved

The results show that this implementation did not deliver the intended benefit. They do not isolate a single cause. The archive leaves several questions open: how much error comes from repeatedly conditioning on generated latents, whether masked training transfers well to fully masked rollout, and whether the representation preserves the fine contact geometry needed for manipulation.

Resolving those questions would require controlled comparisons: matched training budgets, chunk-size and reveal-step ablations, a direct multi-step predictor, and evaluations across seeds. The existing records do not establish that chunked world models are inherently worse, or that more training would fix this one.

## Why this page exists

DA-JEPA did not become the result I wanted. It still became a concrete attempt: a question written down, a model implemented, experiments run, and evidence that challenged the original intuition.

I want my homepage to have room for that part of research too. This page keeps the idea and its outcome together, as a small memorial to a summer of hierarchical imagination.
